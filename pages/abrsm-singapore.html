What is ABRSM? The short answer

ABRSM stands for the Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music — a UK exam board founded in 1889 that now examines students in over 90 countries. Its graded music exams are the most widely recognised music qualification in Singapore, and are regularly used as evidence of musical ability in DSA applications and school music programmes.

Students progress through an Initial Grade, then Grades 1 to 8 — nine graded levels — and can continue to diplomas afterwards. Practical exams are marked out of 150: 100 to pass, 120 for Merit, 130 for Distinction. Before sitting Grade 6, students must first pass Grade 5 Theory (or an approved alternative).

Exam Formats

What are the three types of ABRSM exam?

ABRSM offers three separate exam formats — Practical Grades (face to face, full musicianship), Performance Grades (video-recorded, pieces only) and Music Theory (written). Students often combine them, and choosing the right format makes a real difference to how confident a child feels on exam day.

🎹
Face to face

Practical Grades

The traditional exam, taken in person with an examiner. It assesses complete musicianship, not just prepared pieces.

  • Prepared pieces
  • Scales & arpeggios — 21 marks
  • Sight-reading — 21 marks
  • Aural tests — 18 marks
  • Marked out of 150
🎬
Video submission

Performance Grades

Assessed from a video recording, so your child performs in a familiar room without an examiner watching live. Ideal for nervous performers.

  • 4 pieces — 3 from syllabus + 1 own choice
  • No scales, sight-reading or aural
  • Recorded in one continuous take
  • Marked out of 150 — same bands
📝
Written / online

Music Theory

The written side of music — notation, rhythm, keys, chords and score reading. Grade 5 is the one that unlocks the higher practical grades.

  • Marked out of 75
  • Pass 50 · Merit 60 · Distinction 65
  • Grade 5 required before Practical 6

The Ladder

How many ABRSM grades are there?

There are nine graded levels — an Initial Grade, then Grades 1 through 8 — followed by diploma qualifications (ARSM, DipABRSM, LRSM, FRSM). Grade 8 is the highest graded exam. Grades measure skill, not age: a student is entered when they're genuinely ready, not because they've hit a birthday.

Initial Grade

The very first exam step — three short pieces plus simple scales, sight-reading and aural, at a gentle, confidence-building level. No prerequisites.

Grade 2 — Beginner

Grade 4 — Intermediate

Grade 7 — Advanced

Grade 8 — Highest Grade

The highest graded exam and a widely recognised benchmark — often used as supporting evidence for DSA and SOTA applications. Grade 5 Theory prerequisite applies.

Diplomas — Beyond Grade 8

ARSM, DipABRSM, LRSM and FRSM are advanced performance and teaching qualifications for musicians who continue past Grade 8.

Hover over (or tap) any grade to see what it involves. Grade 5 is highlighted because it's the theory gateway, not because it's a starting point — students may begin at any grade, and Grade 5 Theory must be passed before Grades 6, 7 and 8 can be entered.

🔑

The Grade 5 Theory rule — the one most families get caught by

There are no entry requirements for the Initial Grade and Grades 1 to 5 — your child can start wherever they're ready. But to book a Practical or Performance Grade 6, 7 or 8, they must already have passed Grade 5 or above in Music Theory (or Practical Musicianship, or a permitted solo jazz grade).

This catches families out every year: a student is technically ready for Grade 6 piano but can't enter, because theory was left until the last minute. We start theory alongside practical from around Grade 3 so the certificate is banked long before it's needed.

Results

What marks do you need to pass an ABRSM exam?

Practical and Performance Grades are marked out of 150 — a Pass is 100, a Merit is 120, and a Distinction is 130. Music Theory is marked out of 75 — Pass 50, Merit 60, Distinction 65. Every result comes back in the same three bands.

Exam type Total marks Pass Merit Distinction
Practical Grades 150 100–119 120–129 130–150
Performance Grades 150 100–119 120–129 130–150
Music Theory 75 50+ 60+ 65+

Within Practical Grades, marks are allocated as: scales and arpeggios 21, sight-reading 21, aural tests 18, with the remainder across the prepared pieces. Full marking criteria are published in each subject's qualification specification on the ABRSM website. At Liberal, students have achieved a 99% pass rate across ABRSM and Trinity exams with 200+ Distinctions since 2009 — largely by treating those 60 non-piece marks as seriously as the pieces themselves.

Choosing a Board

ABRSM vs Trinity vs Rockschool: which exam board?

ABRSM is the classical benchmark, Trinity College London (TCL) offers flexible classical and contemporary pathways, and RSL Rockschool is built for rock and pop repertoire. All three are internationally recognised — the right choice depends on the instrument and the student's musical taste, not on prestige.

ABRSM Trinity (TCL) Rockschool (RSL)
Repertoire focus Classical & traditional Classical with contemporary options Rock, pop & contemporary
Theory prerequisite Grade 5 Theory required before Practical / Performance Grades 6–8 No theory prerequisite for higher grades No theory prerequisite for higher grades
Grade range Initial + Grades 1–8, then diplomas Initial + Grades 1–8, then diplomas Debut + Grades 1–8, then diplomas
Typical pathway at Liberal Piano · Violin · Vocal · Theory · Aural Vocal · Drums · flexible pathways Drums · Guitar · contemporary players

This is why our guitar and drum students usually sit Trinity or Rockschool rather than ABRSM, while piano, violin and vocal students most often follow ABRSM. Our teachers recommend the board that fits each student — not the other way round.

Booking

When are the ABRSM exam dates in Singapore?

ABRSM runs several exam sessions in Singapore each year, and entry typically closes around two months before each session. Dates and fees are set directly by ABRSM and revised annually — so plan the entry window early, especially if Grade 5 Theory still needs to be banked first.

Step by Step

How do you enter an ABRSM exam in Singapore?

  1. Choose the exam format. Practical (face to face, full musicianship), Performance (video-recorded, pieces only) or Music Theory (written). Your teacher will recommend the format that suits your child's temperament and goals.
  2. Check the prerequisite. Entering Grade 6, 7 or 8? Grade 5 Theory must already be passed. If not, schedule the theory exam first — it's the step families most often leave too late.
  3. Pick an exam session. Several run in Singapore each year, and entry closes roughly two months ahead — confirm the exact window on the official ABRSM dates page.
  4. Book the entry. Entries are made through ABRSM's online booking. At Liberal, our teachers guide families through the entry so grade, session and format are all correct.
  5. Prepare the full syllabus. Pieces, scales, sight-reading and aural — plus mock exams and recital stage-time at Liberal, so exam day isn't the first time your child performs under pressure.

FAQ

ABRSM questions Singapore parents ask us

The questions that come up most often at trial lessons — answered plainly.

What is ABRSM and is it recognised in Singapore?
ABRSM stands for the Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music, a UK examination board founded in 1889 that now examines students in more than 90 countries. Its graded music exams are among the most widely recognised music qualifications in Singapore and are commonly used to evidence musical ability for DSA applications and school music programmes.
How many ABRSM grades are there?
ABRSM offers an Initial Grade followed by Grades 1 to 8 — nine graded levels in total. Beyond Grade 8, students can progress to diploma qualifications: ARSM, DipABRSM, LRSM and FRSM.
What is the highest ABRSM grade?
Grade 8 is the highest graded ABRSM exam. After Grade 8, students can continue to professional diplomas — ARSM, DipABRSM, LRSM and FRSM — which are advanced qualifications beyond the graded system rather than graded exams.
Do I need to pass Grade 5 Theory before Grade 6 practical?
Yes. Before booking a Practical or Performance Grade 6, 7 or 8 exam, candidates must first have passed ABRSM Grade 5 (or above) in Music Theory, Practical Musicianship, or a permitted solo jazz instrument grade. There are no entry requirements for Initial Grade or Grades 1 to 5.
What marks do you need to pass an ABRSM exam?
Practical Grades and Performance Grades are marked out of 150. A Pass is 100 to 119 marks, a Merit is 120 to 129, and a Distinction is 130 to 150. Music Theory exams are marked out of 75, where a Pass is 50, a Merit is 60 and a Distinction is 65.
What is the difference between ABRSM Practical Grades and Performance Grades?
Practical Grades are taken face to face and assess prepared pieces plus scales and arpeggios (21 marks), sight-reading (21 marks) and aural tests (18 marks). Performance Grades are assessed from a video recording and consist of four pieces, three from the syllabus and one own-choice piece, with no scales, sight-reading or aural tests. Both are marked out of 150 using the same result bands.
What are ABRSM aural tests?
Aural tests are short listening exercises included in every Practical Grade exam, worth 18 of the 150 marks. Depending on the grade, students clap rhythms, sing back melodies, and answer questions about music the examiner plays. Because they are often left unpractised, aural tests are one of the most common places students lose marks — which is why Liberal builds aural training into weekly lessons.
When are the ABRSM exam dates in Singapore?
ABRSM runs several exam sessions in Singapore each year, and entry typically closes around two months before each session. Exact dates and fees are set by ABRSM and change annually, so always check the official ABRSM exam dates and fees page before planning. Our teachers help families choose the right session and schedule Grade 5 Theory in time.
How long does it take to prepare for an ABRSM grade?
Most students at Liberal Music & Arts School take roughly nine to twelve months per grade at the lower levels with consistent weekly lessons and regular home practice, and longer at Grade 6 and above. Timelines vary with the student's starting level, practice consistency and age, so our teachers set an individual target grade and exam window after the first trial lesson.
What is Liberal's ABRSM pass rate?
Students at Liberal Music & Arts School have achieved a 99% pass rate across ABRSM and Trinity exams, including more than 200 ABRSM Distinctions, since 2009. We reach this by entering students only when they are genuinely ready, and by training aural, sight-reading and theory alongside exam pieces from the start.

Do guitar and drum students take ABRSM exams too?
Guitar and drum students usually follow Trinity Rock & Pop or RSL Rockschool syllabuses rather than ABRSM, since those boards are designed for contemporary and rock repertoire. ABRSM remains the most common pathway for piano, violin, vocal and music theory. Our teachers recommend the board that best fits each student's instrument and goals.
